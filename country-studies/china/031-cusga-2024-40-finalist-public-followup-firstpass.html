0. Execution and conclusion boundaries

Unlike winner/media-based case selection, this round began from all 40 project-title + team pairs in the reconstructed finalist roster and issued one public search "<title>" "<public team alias>" [game/2025/2026] per baseline entry in category batches, then cross-checked identifiable leads against Steam primary storefront, itch project, official studio self-publishing, developer’s public 2024 documentation and contemporaneous alumni interviews. This is FIRST_PASS_DISCOVERY and not exhaustive result verification for all 40 across Steam/itch/GmHub/TapTap/B站; search coverage and ambiguity are asymmetric. Search engines favor store pages and featured games.

What can already be stated at this evidence threshold: - 8/40 have high-confidence original-project Steam records, not necessarily new, completed or released after the contest: F03 F08 F12 F13 F15 F23 F34 F39. - 2/40 have Steam titles and similar gameplay, but original public team aliases do not directly align (F09 F32): ALIAS_RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED and excluded from strict 8 until official developer lineage is established. - 1/40 has itch downloadable Windows/macOS evidence, with creator continuity through original published production credits (F33): it is public distribution/continued build, not a Steam commercial sale. - 2 of those 8 Steam projects were already released before T0: F12《彼方的她》（2024-04-25）and F39《随机军团》（2024-05-13）. Therefore treating all current released projects as “after-finals conversion” is retroactive attribution. - 1 possible alias project F32 is freeware released 2024-12-25 but is not yet eligible for a confirmed-same-team release rate. - 1 2024 competing project F02 has confirmed partial alumni creator branching into a distinct project, but original game release remains UNVERIFIED, and most members’ futures UNKNOWN. - No verified match in this first batch is NOT a negative outcome. We did not calculate abandonment, 2-year retention, paid release fraction, employment rates, national creator formation rates.

3. First-pass bias and what it invalidates

  • Steam store discovery far outperforms GmHub, Bilibili small uploads and nonindexed itch under the current search approach; apparent “published globally” share is a visibility lower bound, not an intention measure.
  • Already released games enter student competitions. Such competitions select quality and maturity, and later games may have been invested in long before nomination. “Award-to-launch success rate” needs T0 before first market access, or at least competing risks/left truncation.
  • Award prize compared with student submission is a selection filter; 40 finalists are too selected to estimate ordinary student innovation awareness. Even follow-up complete 40/40 will only describe this particular selected finalist cohort.
  • A studio can have one successful marketed game and other team members can work elsewhere; don’t collapse game project, team and named creator into one longitudinal entity.
  • Paid release, free finished release, downloadable prototype, Demo, store page without date, company formation and investment are distinct evidence gates.
  • CUSGA 2024 has nonmainland eligible participants; without each baseline’s region, even the selected 40 project cross-region “China rate” is not yet defensible.

  4. Establish region and pre-contest product maturity for each participating team, otherwise no Taiwan vs Mainland conversion difference.
  5. Sample normal student/consumer pre-entry populations to compare FIRST_ATTEMPT_ACCESS, and creator self-report for post-award employment only with consent.

5. Source and validity standard

Direct primary storefront data P0; 2024 CUSGA organizer/winner award announcements P0; contemporaneous creator content P0 for self-described art & process, not audited company economics; third-party interview S1 with creator first-person P0/P1 depending retrospective timing. Source snapshots may change. No report of a Steam game’s 2026 user review total as exact market revenue, no claim of causality.

This sheet is a verified first-pass source intake, not a completed longitudinal career census.
